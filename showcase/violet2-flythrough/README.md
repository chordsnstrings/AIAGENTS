# Violet 2 — TH12-4M townhouse flythrough (three.js)

Scroll-driven, real-time 3D flythrough of **plot XJ407B** (unit `VOT2/SD143/XJ407B`), a
four-bedroom **TH12-4M** mid-terrace townhouse in **Violet 2, DAMAC Hills 2, Dubai**.
The camera behaves like a stabilised drone: an aerial approach over the community, an
orbit to the street, then through the front door and every room, a reverse pull-out over
the garden, up the stair core to the roof terrace at sunset, and a blue-hour pull-back that
leaves the lit house on screen beside the summary.

```bash
npm install
npm run build     # bundles src/ -> dist/app.js (three.js r187 + n8ao)
npm run serve     # http://localhost:8080
```

Open `index.html` through any static server (module scripts do not run from `file://`).

## What is real and what is illustrative

Real, from the developer documents (sales offer + Violet brochure p.27):
plot 6.10 × 18.50 m (112.85 m²), room positions and sizes, three levels, two covered
parking spaces, saleable areas (589.22 / 632.92 / 296.12 / 834.00 sq ft = 2,352.26 sq ft),
facade language (white render frame, grey stone, bronze railings and lattice screen,
slatted timber pergolas), community drive times and amenities.

Illustrative: furniture and finishes, landscaping, the neighbouring cluster layout
(curving back-to-back rows modelled on the sales-offer location plan), lagoon and dunes.
The page says so in its summary card. Price and payment plan are deliberately not shown.

## Structure

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `src/plan.js` | Single source of truth: plan coordinates (x across plot, d from street, z = -d), levels, rooms. Drives both the 3D model and the SVG mini-plans. |
| `src/builder.js` | Accumulates boxes/quads/geometry per material key and emits one merged mesh (or one `InstancedMesh`) per material. Box UVs are in metres so textures tile at real-world scale. |
| `src/house.js` | Exterior shell of one townhouse. Hero copy has real balusters and open doors; neighbours get alpha railing cards and fake lit interiors. |
| `src/interior.js` | Hero-only interior: floors, linings, stacked dog-leg stair, doors, kitchen, bathrooms, furniture, interior lights. |
| `src/community.js` | Curving rows of 7-unit blocks around the hero (mirrored alternates), LOD blocks with a facade atlas further out, streets, lamps and their baked ground-light map, palms, lagoon, desert; the rooftop kit (pavers, coping, condensers, tanks) every house wears. |
| `src/materials.js` | PBR sets from CC0 photo-scans (Poly Haven, ambientCG) packed as WebP colour / normal / AO-roughness; every finish named by the albedo it should read as; shader patches for tile joints, macro variation (a tiling noise texture), texture contrast, fresnel glass, lamp-shade and opal-bulb glow, palm sway, the baked-GI lookup, the street-lamp irradiance map and a point-light-free variant for exterior finishes. |
| `src/gi.js`, `assets/gi/*.json` | Baked irradiance volume (generated by `scripts/bake-gi.mjs`; do not edit the data files) fetched beside the textures and mixed per moment into two 3D textures; one lamp set per style, decoded when the style shows. |
| `src/look.js` | The photographic constants: indoor exposure, GI fill and lamp weights, white balance, look-in and look-out weights, the night lamp pool, bloom ramp, street-lamp level, dust brightness. |
| `src/proctex.js` | Canvas-drawn textures: palm fronds, foliage and flower cards, lattice and railing alpha, abstract art, lit and dim room quads, the facade atlas, water normals. |
| `src/vegetation.js` | Palm geometry at two detail levels, card bushes, and the instancing helper every repeated object uses. |
| `src/softgoods.js` | Procedural duvets, pillows, throws and sheers: smooth noise plus a drape rule, no runtime simulation. |
| `src/atmosphere.js` | Preetham sky with lit clouds, sun + shadow zones, PMREM environment from the sky assigned to every lit material, two reflection probes for the glazing, fog, stars, time of day. |
| `src/path.js` | Keyframed drone route: positions on a centripetal Catmull-Rom, orientation (yaw, pitch), fov and lens shift on monotone cubics; gimbal rig (lag, bank, hover, view offset). |
| `src/story.js` | The one chapter table: card windows, rail labels, where a rail click lands, the day-to-night ramp, the sun swing and the plot-pin windows. |
| `src/tiers.js` | Quality tiers as pixel budgets, GPU classification from the renderer name, the adaptive resolution scaler. |
| `src/post.js` | N8AO ambient occlusion, volumetric light, bloom, ACES output, grade/vignette/grain, SMAA. |
| `src/volumetric.js` | Shadow-map raymarched light shafts and haze, dust motes, lamp cones. |
| `src/styles.js`, `src/stylesets.js` | Interior style presets (materials, named by the albedo a finish should read as) and their furniture sets. |
| `src/ui.js` | Loader, chapter cards, rail, mini-plans, printable plan sheet, plot pin, keyboard chapter navigation, live region. |

## Route and chapters

`src/story.js` holds the thirteen chapters with their progress windows; `src/path.js` keys the
camera to the same numbers, and `index.html` cards name their chapter with `data-chapter`.
Changing a chapter's timing is a two-file edit (table and keys); the cards, rail, time-of-day
ramp, sun swing and pin follow.

| Chapter | Progress | Shot |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival | 0–0.04 | aerial from about 110 m, the house centred and pinned from the first frame, the hero rows and the lagoon in frame |
| The community | 0.056–0.13 | descent over the lakes and terraces to the plot |
| Facade | 0.146–0.215 | orbit down to the street in front of the house |
| Ground floor | 0.235–0.31 | carport, front door, a glance right through the kitchen opening, the hall |
| Living | 0.335–0.452 | an arc along the living room looking at the sofa, table and glazing, turning back towards the room |
| Garden | 0.474–0.505 | reverse pull-out through the open panel to a hold over the rear wall: patio, lit glazing, balcony |
| Bedrooms | 0.521–0.58 | in from the rear balcony to a level hold just inside the glazing: bed, prints, lamps, the wardrobe wall by the door |
| Master suite | 0.602–0.664 | through the door to a 3/4 view from the foot of the bed, the front glazing, sheers and lattice as the key light |
| Front balcony | 0.68–0.712 | out through the open panel to a hold at the balustrade over the two loungers, lattice at the left, then a reverse dolly back through the room |
| The stair | 0.728–0.784 | back into the well and up over the half landing to a hold on the dog-leg: the upper flight and its rail climbing to the roof landing and its window, the first-floor hall below; then up to the landing |
| Bedroom 4 | 0.80–0.866 | from the stair top through the doorway past the open leaf to a hold inside: bed, prints, the terrace through the glazing |
| Roof terrace | 0.882–0.944 | out through the glazing before the card arrives, then round to the sofa, loungers and fire bowl under the pergola with the sun going down |
| Summary | 0.96–1 | rise and pull back to the right; the lit rear elevation at blue hour |

Rules the route obeys, enforced by `node scripts/check-path.mjs` (exit 1 on a breach): lens at
least 0.3 m from any solid, authored look targets at least 2.4 m from the lens, pitch −35..+40°,
at most 12° of yaw and 6° of pitch per 0.005 of progress, lens shift within ±0.5 of the frame
height, consecutive card windows at least 0.016 apart, no uncaptioned stretch over 0.03, and
every rail landing inside its window. Orientation is interpolated as yaw and pitch, never as a
look point, so a target can never pass beside the lens and flip the view. Interiors stay level:
where a bed should sit low in the frame the key carries a vertical lens shift
(`camera.setViewOffset`) instead of a downward tilt, so verticals stay straight.

## Controls

- Scroll, trackpad or touch drives the flight; the rail on the right lists the chapters and a
  click lands on each chapter's best frame.
- Keyboard: `→` `↓` `PageDown` `Space` next chapter, `←` `↑` `PageUp` `Shift+Space` previous,
  `Home` / `End` the ends. A key pressed while the flight is still easing towards a landing
  steps on from that landing, so two quick presses move two chapters.
- Screen readers: the active chapter is announced, and the whole story (every card's text,
  figures and the disclaimer) sits in a visually hidden section that a **Skip to the chapter
  text** link, the first stop for the Tab key, reaches. The inactive cards are hidden from
  assistive tech, so without it only the current chapter could be read.
- Top right: **Day → Night** (the light follows the flight: daylight, sunset on the roof,
  night by the summary), **Day** or **Night** throughout; the **Interior** style pill shows
  from the facade to the roof terrace, where a change is visible, and the choice is kept in
  the URL hash (`#japandi`).
- The summary offers **Replay**, **Copy link to this style** and **Print the plans** (a plan
  sheet of the three floors drawn from `src/plan.js`, for print or save-as-PDF).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` removes the hover, bank and easing lag so the view follows the
  scroll directly. Without WebGL the loader stays as a page with the poster and the facts.

## Interior styles

Four presets researched from 2026 trend reporting (Dezeen, 1stDibs designer survey, Khaleej
Times on Dubai "restrained luxury", Bayut UAE trends, Domus Academy, paint colours of the
year): **Warm Minimal**, **Japandi Calm**, **Desert Organic**, **Aubergine Luxe**. A preset
re-parameterises shared material roles across the whole house (`src/styles.js`) and shows
its own living, dining and master-bedroom furniture (`src/stylesets.js`). Material types
never change, so switching recompiles nothing; a style's extra textures load on first use.
Every style keeps light walls and ceilings and sets a contrasting `joinery` role for doors,
wardrobes and kitchen runs (walnut, smoked black ash, caramel elm-oak, smoked walnut).
Deep link with `#japandi`, `#desert`, `#luxe` (or `?style=` for captures).

Every textured role names its finish by **albedo**, the sRGB colour it should read as: the styler
divides the scan's measured mean (`SET_AVG`) out of it, so a white duvet renders white rather than
the scan's grey times a tint, and the GI bake lights the rooms with the same numbers. A role's
`rough` is likewise the finish's real mean roughness: three multiplies the material roughness by
the packed roughness map, whose mean (`SET_ROUGH`) is divided out, so honed stone no longer turns
into a mirror of the sky. Velvet roles add `flat`, which pulls the crushed scan towards the colour
so the sheen, not the texture noise, reads as velvet. A style switch resolves every texture first
and the last click wins, so a failed download leaves nothing half-applied.

## Lighting and the look

Golden hour to blue hour is three keyframes in `src/atmosphere.js` (a dust-hazed Gulf sky:
turbidity 8, Rayleigh 2.6, a broad Mie lobe, a few high wisps; in three's Preetham model more
Rayleigh is what pales the blue, since the blue channel's extinction saturates first, and the gain
holds the sky's irradiance where it was); the sun holds still per zone so the shadow map renders
once. The environment map is assigned to every lit material explicitly,
so per-material and per-style `envMapIntensity` values count; environments are cached as render
targets (LRU, the bake's reference pinned) and the asynchronous sky read-back behind the baked
light is validated before it is trusted. Street-front and garden-facing glazing mirror two local
reflection probes (256 px cube renders, refreshed on load and at steps of the evening), so windows
show the street and the garden instead of reading as holes. Lamp shades are linen over the bulb:
the shade shader grades the glow from the rim to the top and burns hotter on the inside, opal
globes darken towards their rim, and every bulb position is recorded (`interior.lamps`, each
style kit's `lamps`) for a pool of night point lights; there are no ceiling-level fill lights, so
night rooms are lamp-lit pools rather than an even wash (only the terrace fire, which the bake does
not carry, has a point light of its own). Exposure follows the frame, read from a 5 x 3 grid of
rays: indoors it opens up about two stops and gives part of that back in proportion to how much of
the frame looks out through sunlit glazing within reach; looking in from outside it opens up about
a stop in proportion to how much of the frame is room; behind the house it lifts for the shaded
rear elevation. The
photographic numbers (indoor exposure, fill, lamp level, look-in and look-out weights, garden shade
lift, bloom ramp and cap) live in `src/look.js`.

## Baked global illumination

`scripts/bake-gi.mjs` (Node + `three-mesh-bvh`, ~12 min at 256 rays) ray-traces an L1
spherical-harmonic irradiance volume at 0.5 m over the hero plot, using the page's own
geometry and finish albedos: sky light from the same Preetham model, sun light bounced off
surfaces for both the held sun (south-west, 18°) and the swung sun of the terrace chapters
(north-west, 11.5°), and each style's lamps as soft point emitters; two bounces each, probes
inside walls refilled from neighbours. It writes `assets/gi/S.json`, `U.json`, `U2.json` and
`N-<style>.json` (base64 bytes with the grid's metadata): the page fetches them beside the
textures, decodes a style's lamp set only when that style shows, and mixes the parts for the
moment (sky as the live env map measures it, the two sun bounces cross-faded with the swing,
lamps as dusk falls). Where the volume says a point is enclosed its irradiance replaces the env
map's diffuse light, and the env reflections are dimmed by the probe's open-sky fraction and
tinted by the room's own bounce colour, with what the walls send back (a mid albedo times the
irradiance) filling the part of the reflection the sky does not, so brass and other metals in a
closed room mirror the room instead of reading black; outdoors the env map stays in charge. Interiors are then
exposed about two stops brighter by day, as an interiors photographer would, with lamps on
dimmed. Re-run the bake after moving walls, furniture or lamps:

```bash
npm install && npm run bake   # three-mesh-bvh is a dev dependency
```

## Volumetrics

`src/volumetric.js`: a half-resolution raymarch through the sun's static shadow map gives
shafts through glazing, pergola slats and palms, and a height-weighted haze; density is
per sample (dusty air inside the house volume, thin haze outside; the open roof terrace counts
as outside). Dust motes sparkle only
where the shadow map says sunlight reaches them; lamp cones fade in at dusk. The sun holds
south-west through the master suite, swings north-west for the terrace and sets at the
sunset view so the rays meet the visible sun.

## Performance decisions

- Three quality tiers are pixel budgets, not device-pixel-ratio caps (`src/tiers.js`): `high`
  3.7 MP, `mid` 2.1 MP, `low` 0.6 MP of device pixels per frame. The tier comes from the GPU name
  (`WEBGL_debug_renderer_info`): discrete GPUs and Apple Pro/Max chips `high`, integrated laptop
  GPUs, base M1/M2 and iPads `mid`, phones and software renderers `low`; `?q=high|mid|low`
  overrides it. The pixel ratio follows from the budget and the canvas box, and an adaptive
  scaler walks rungs of 0.6–1.3× that ratio on measured frame cost (the GPU timer where the
  browser offers one), stepping up only once the scroll has settled.
- Sun direction is fixed; the shadow map (2048² on every tier) renders once per zone (aerial
  600 m, street 120 m, house 36 m) and during the terrace sun swing. Far houses and far palms
  cast only from the air, so the swing redraws about 0.4 M caster triangles instead of 1.1 M.
- Palms are two instanced models, 1,112 and 192 triangles, split at 70 m from the camera and
  re-partitioned once it has moved 5 m. A frame is 0.80–0.89 M triangles in 157–186 draw calls
  (software renderer, 1024 × 576): per-material merging for the hero, instancing for every
  neighbour, rooftop condenser, palm and shrub, opaque community meshes drawn after the hero so
  interiors are not shaded twice.
- The bundle is 994 KB minified (338 KB gzip, from 1,407 KB / 576 KB) because the baked light no
  longer travels in it: `assets/gi/*.json` (seven files, 490 KB, 286 KB gzip) download beside
  the textures, starting before them, and only the style on screen is decoded; a style switch
  fetches its own lamp set. Serve with brotli or gzip; the JSON is base64 and compresses by half.
- Exterior finishes (ground, far houses, bark, fronds) compile without the point-light loop
  (`noPoint`), so the four interior lights and the three-light night pool cost nothing on the
  80 % of the scroll that looks at the estate; the macro variation on the ground is two fetches
  of a tiling noise texture instead of sixteen sine hashes per pixel. Night street light is one
  baked irradiance map (R8, 0.24 m per texel) sampled by the ground materials, not stacked
  additive quads. Glazing is single-sided; the sheers write depth so glass behind a curtain is
  rejected rather than composited.
- The night point lights are a fixed pool of three parked on the bulbs nearest the camera and
  cross-faded when they move, so the light count, and with it every program, never changes.
- Environment maps are binned (time of day in tenths, the sun swing in three azimuth steps,
  elevation in 4° steps; coarser on phones), kept in an LRU of 16 (6 on phones) with the bake's
  reference pinned, and prebuilt one per idle moment for the evening once the page is up, so the
  sunset never stalls on a PMREM render.
- The post chain is two full-resolution passes: the scene through N8AO (a plain render pass on
  `low` outside the house and street zones) and one finish pass that adds the half-resolution
  volumetric march and the bloom in linear light, then tone-maps, encodes and grades. SMAA runs
  only while the pixel ratio is under 1.75; `low` skips the volumetric march. The bloom
  threshold rides with the exposure (`2.5 / exposure`) so lamps halo at night and sunlit render
  does not bloom by day, and only the excess over the threshold blooms, capped at one threshold
  (`LOOK.bloomCap`): passed whole, a low sun's Mie glow bloomed into a white veil over the roof
  terrace.
- Textures: 18 base sets; the 11 exterior ones are awaited, the 7 interior-only sets stream in
  behind the first frame (the camera starts in the air). Phones read `assets/tex/512/`
  (3.0 MB for all 29 sets against 8.6 MB), about 58 MiB of texture memory instead of 232 MiB,
  and release the style-only sets they no longer show.
- Rendering sleeps when the scroll has settled. Indoor light is two 3D texture fetches per
  pixel instead of area lights, re-mixed on the CPU with weights quantised to 1/128, at most
  every third frame while the light eases.
- The drawing buffer, camera and overlay are sized from the canvas box (the large viewport)
  through a `ResizeObserver` with a 150 ms debounce, so a phone's toolbar never reallocates the
  render targets mid-scroll. A lost WebGL context shows a notice and reloads on restore.
- Debug/capture: `?p=0.42` renders a single still at that progress (every texture set is
  awaited, so stills are complete); `?tod=night` forces night; `&ui=0` hides the overlay;
  `&cam=x,y,z,lx,ly,lz[,fov[,shift]]` places the camera directly (with `?p=`) to compose a
  shot before writing it into `src/path.js`; `&hide=sheer,lampBeams,dust` hides scene objects
  by name to tell what a stray highlight is, and `&off=vol,bloom` drops the volumetric march or
  the bloom from the finish composite to tell which of them makes a veil. Regenerate the 512 tier after re-packing sets with
  the skill's `textures.py --downscale 512`: Lanczos to 512 px, WebP quality 82, method 6,
  files already at 512 px copied.
